# Ethics: Amanuensimus

This document is not appended ethics — it is the philosophical position the framework encodes. If the technical work is separated from these commitments, it becomes just another AI-writing tool, indistinguishable from the ones that produce the AI-slop flood Amanuensimus exists in opposition to.

## The core stance

**Attribution by responsibility, not by causal chain.**

Ideas are never wholly internal. The "arranger who completes the pattern" test — is this yours because you placed the finishing piece? — has always been the real test of authorship, historically applied to writers whose components came from prior reading, conversation, and cultural inheritance. AI-collaborative authorship is a new instance of an old pattern, not a novel category.

What survives the collapse of "voice vs ideas" is a cleaner criterion for whether something is yours:

- Can you defend it under scrutiny you didn't rehearse for?
- Can you apply it to adjacent cases you didn't discuss with the AI?
- Do you understand its dependencies well enough to notice when they don't hold?
- Would you take responsibility for its consequences?

If yes, the causal chain — read, thought, discussed, prompted, iterated, revised — is philosophically uninteresting. The output is yours in every sense that matters. If no, you don't really have the idea; you have the appearance of it, and the gap will show under any competent questioning.

This is a stricter bar than "I typed it myself" but a more honest one. It applies to AI-assisted work, uncritical reading, badly-remembered lectures, and undigested collaboration equally.

## The philosophical grounding

Clark, A. and Chalmers, D. (1998) 'The Extended Mind', *Analysis*, 58(1), pp. 7–19.

Cognition is not fully bounded by the skull. A notebook that reliably holds your appointments is functionally part of your memory. A conversation partner who reliably completes your half-formed thoughts is functionally part of your thinking. AI collaboration instantiates this thesis at a scale and rate that makes the fiction of the isolated thinker impossible to sustain — which is a feature, not a bug. It makes us honest about a picture of cognition that was already true.

The tradition since Clark and Chalmers: 4E cognition (embodied, embedded, enacted, extended); Karen Barad on the ontology of collaboration; Evan Thompson on the phenomenology of thought. AI-collaborative cognition connects to what's already there. Amanuensimus is one instance of tooling that makes the connection legible in practice.

The neurodivergent-cognition vantage adds a specific claim to this tradition: cognitive scaffolding is not a compensation for atypical minds; it is the actual shape of *all* cognition, more visible in some brains than in others. Neurodivergent cognition makes visible the scaffolds neurotypical cognition uses invisibly and calls "just thinking". Writing from that vantage is not applying philosophy to a special case — it is contributing evidence the philosophy has been under-served by.

## The ghostwriter analogy

Ghostwriting is longstanding, disclosed (when done honestly), and respectable — autobiographies, speeches, corporate communications. The ethical framework maps cleanly onto AI-assisted prose:

- Disclosed → legitimate
- Undisclosed → deception
- Ideas attribution stays with the credited author; the ghostwriter helps render
- Readers judge the ideas, knowing the voice may be a collaboration

The competing "word processor" analogy is doing more work than it can bear. A word processor is a transcription tool — it encodes thoughts you already had. An LLM is *compositional* — it proposes phrasings you hadn't yet composed. Ghostwriter is the analogy that survives sceptical questioning.

## Three uncomfortable implications

These belong in the ethics document because ducking them would be dishonest.

**1. Extended mind, at its limit, does not distinguish authorship from imitation.**

If reading X and being changed by X counts as ideation, then sophisticated imitation — internalising an author's patterns until you produce indistinguishable work — differs from authorship only in the responsibility criterion above. Which is right. That is the actual distinction and it always was. Plagiarism is wrong not because you re-used words; it is wrong because you can't defend, extend, or take responsibility for them in the way authorship requires. The extended-mind frame clarifies both the wrongness of plagiarism and the rightness of AI-assisted authorship — *by the same test*.

**2. Not all AI-assisted output is equally yours.**

Content accepted verbatim without engagement is yours in a thinner sense than content argued with, iterated on, reshaped. The synthesis-vs-generation distinction operates within AI collaboration too. Ownership is not binary; it is textured. Amanuensimus's provenance manifest reflects this — recording not just what was generated, but how it was engaged with.

**3. Responsibility cuts against the author, not for them.**

The collaboration frame does not lower the accountability bar; it raises it. The author gets the credit *and* the labour was distributed *and* the consequences are theirs *and* they had every opportunity to check. "The AI misled me" is not a defence — it is evidence of failure to notice. Anyone arguing for AI-assisted authorship on principled grounds is accepting *stricter* liability, not laxer. This is worth being explicit about, because it is also the strongest rebuttal to bad-faith readings of the position.

## The disclosure practice

Baseline reference: **section 1.2 of `hand-rolled-secp256k1-lit-review/review-final.md`.** The elements that make it exemplary:

1. Names the specific model and vendor (Claude Opus 4.6, Anthropic 2025–2026)
2. Distinguishes author work (research questions, source selection methodology, evidence weighting, conclusions) from model work (prose from prompts)
3. Describes the review process (adversarial verification, balance review, domain review — each in *fresh sessions* with no cross-contamination)
4. Takes explicit personal responsibility
5. Documents TOS/usage-policy review
6. Notes data handling (no personal or proprietary content submitted)
7. Additionally discloses an abandoned prior version whose bias-artefact trail is preserved as research evidence

Amanuensimus generates disclosure to this standard by default, adapted per venue.

**Refined authorship framing** (evolved from the older "ideas are my own"):

> I take full responsibility for the framework, the arguments, and the conclusions. The prose and structural rendering are the product of collaboration with a model, iteratively directed and reviewed. Attribution follows responsibility, not authorship of individual sentences.

Same substance as section 1.2; more honest metaphysics. Less "I did this, the model helped"; more "we did this, I answer for it". The latter is closer to how it actually worked and is easier to defend under adversarial questioning because it stops trying to draw a line that doesn't hold up.

## Reader-side integrity

The compact only holds if readers accept parallel responsibility. Amanuensimus explicitly builds reader-side tooling because without it, the authorship norms are stranded.

**The reader's responsibility test** parallels the author's:

- Have you engaged enough to defend your citation of it?
- Would you notice if an AI summary distorted the original?
- Can you recognise when the argument doesn't apply to your case?

If yes — AI-assisted reading is legitimate, disclosed, valid. If no — you're pretending to have engaged with something you haven't, which is the same failure mode as an author pretending to have written what they didn't.

**The paired reader-side disclosure template:**

> I engaged with this work through a combination of direct reading and AI-assisted analysis. My use of its arguments and citations is defensible; I take responsibility for having verified what I rely on.

## The gatekeeping observation

The common objection *"if you don't have time to write it, I don't have time to read it"* is not primarily about time. Time is the mask. Labour used to be a proxy for thinking; the proxy is broken. Someone willing to spend 100 hours writing something had almost certainly spent significant time thinking about it. AI decouples labour from thinking. The prose alone can no longer distinguish thought-through work from unthought work.

What people *should* be saying is *"I don't have time to read work whose author hasn't thought about it."* But that criterion is hard to measure directly, so they fall back on the last measurable proxy — "does this look AI-generated?" — which filters on *rendering* rather than *thinking*. Wrong axis entirely.

The symmetric solution is AI-assisted reading: if the concern is attention wisely spent, AI can help the reader filter too — on ideas, not on rendering. That the objection typically does *not* dissolve when this solution is offered reveals it was gatekeeping in filter's clothing — a normative claim about who deserves engagement, disguised as a personal time-management heuristic.

Amanuensimus takes the honest position: build tools for both sides of the compact. The authorship-integrity story is stranded without the corresponding reading-integrity story.

## What Amanuensimus refuses

Concretely:

- **Undisclosed use.** The framework will not generate an artefact without a corresponding provenance manifest and human-readable disclosure. Users can override — nothing is technically enforced against a determined opponent — but the tooling makes disclosure the path of least resistance.
- **Voice cloning without consent.** Voice profiles are built from an author's own corpus with their explicit participation. The framework will not, by default, build a voice profile of a third party from scraped writing.
- **Manipulation-oriented outputs.** Amanuensimus is not for producing text designed to deceive a specific reader about the author's mental state or expertise. The framework aims at *voice fidelity for legitimate collaboration*, not at *fooling humans for adversarial ends*.
- **Bad-faith bias mitigation.** The workflow harness enforces evidence-first methodology and session hygiene. Users can technically bypass it, but the framework's default posture treats bypassing as an integrity failure worth flagging.

Different instances of one underlying thesis: *the AI transition demands new integrity practices on every side of the knowledge-production loop, because the old proxies (labour, familiarity, visible effort) have decoupled from the things they were proxies for.*

The reading paper is arguably the most important of the three, because it addresses the audience whose acceptance the other two depend on. Without new reading norms, the new writing norms are stranded — everyone who accepts your writing practice will be marginal for as long as reading practice hasn't caught up.
