amanuensimus Design ledger

The design is not versioned. It is amended — one token at a time, by the same mechanism the argument uses.

Why not a scale

A scale of design states forces coordinated jumps. Each rung has to land whole, every token moving together, and the work of judging it is the work of judging an entire redesign. That is expensive, brittle, and not how anything gets built.

Worse, it invites a schedule. A schedule commits you to changing things before the reason to change them has arrived, which is how a design acquires decisions nobody can account for.

Amendment, not version

The project already has a mechanism for changing a position without erasing where it came from. The design enrols in it.

An amendment moves one token. It carries a date, a stated reason, and a trigger. It is recorded rather than applied silently, and prior values are kept rather than overwritten.

The question does the intermediate state hold up? then answers itself. Every state is a single delta from a state that already held. There is nothing to synchronise, because there is never more than one thing in motion.

What makes an amendment

  • One token. If it needs two unrelated tokens, it is two amendments.
  • A trigger, not a date. The condition under which the change becomes warranted.
  • A reason. Stated in terms of the work, not of taste.
  • Declared sympathies. The tokens that will need re-checking once this one moves.

Sympathies, and deferring them

Some tokens cannot honestly move alone. Change the face and the measure needs a look, because a different face sets a different number of characters to the em.

The answer is not to bundle them. It is to declare the coupling and let the sympathy be applied later, on its own evidence. Change the face; read it for a fortnight; then shorten the measure because you have now seen it fail, not because a rule said you would.

A deferred sympathy is an open amendment. It sits in the register unapplied, which is a more honest state than a change made on prediction.

What cannot move

Three properties are locked, and are load-bearing rather than merely settled: two columns; the rule at one pixel of full ink; the graft in the second tone.

Two more are held but amendable by a constitutional amendment — no hue, and no more than two tones. Breaking one of those requires a stated reason and a recorded objection, so that the break is legible later as a decision rather than a drift.

Whether the record keeps its setting

If the whole site re-renders at current tokens, it stays coherent and the record of its own evolution is destroyed — which contradicts the position on abandoned versions directly.

If every artefact freezes at the tokens it was issued under, the record survives and the site becomes a patchwork of hands, which is either the point or a mess.

The split that holds: the living statement renders at current tokens, because it is living. Dated artefacts freeze at issue. The top of the site is always now; the archive is stratified, and reading down it is reading the evolution.

This costs almost nothing to implement. Tokens are custom properties, so freezing an artefact is one scoped declaration on its wrapper. Anti-erasure comes free, which is a good sign that the mechanism is the right shape.
