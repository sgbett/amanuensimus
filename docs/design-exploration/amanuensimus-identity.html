¶ i

The wordmark above is already doing the work. amanuensi is the scribe; mus is the tail of animus, grafted on. The seam is disclosed rather than smoothed.

A second reading worth keeping: -imus is the Latin superlative. Amanuensimus also parses as “most scribe-like”.

The organising idea: rubrication

Your hardest identity problem — how do you make a second contributor visible in a text without diminishing the first? — already has an eight-hundred-year-old solution.

Medieval manuscripts were collaborative artefacts. The scribe wrote the body in iron-gall ink and left gaps. A second person, the rubricator, came afterwards and worked into those gaps in red: paragraph marks, initials, corrections, the apparatus that makes a text navigable. Two hands, one page, structurally necessary, never hidden. Nobody thought the rubricator diminished the scribe.

That is disclosure integrity as a visual grammar rather than a logo. It gives the palette a reason instead of a preference, and it means the identity can be applied to a README, a paper, a CLI and a provenance manifest without ever repeating a symbol.

One discipline makes it work: red is rationed. It never appears decoratively. Wherever it appears, it marks the second hand — the model's contribution, the disclosure, the graft. Blue does the ordinary structural work. If red starts appearing in headings and buttons, the whole system collapses into ornament.

This document is set in the system it proposes, so you can judge it in use rather than in the abstract. The paragraph marks in the margin arrive after the text, as they did.

Four marks

Each is drawn from an actual convention for marking a second hand in a text. None is an abstract glyph looking for a meaning.

Developed

A — The Paraph

A pilcrow split between hands: one stem in ink, the bowl and second stem in red. The scribe's line, and the rubricator's mark wrapped around it. Reads at 16 px.

Developed

B — Interpolation

Editorial brackets, opened by one hand and closed by another, deliberately off register. Scholars already read [ ] as “inserted, and marked as such”. Most restrained; most defensible.

Sketch

C — Manicule

The pointing hand readers drew in the margins of books they did not write. Aims at the reader-side pillar. Warmer, more idiosyncratic, and harder to keep serious at small sizes.

The rule that keeps it honest

Minium marks the second hand. Azurite marks the third. If a red element on a page does not correspond to a machine contribution or its disclosure, it should be blue, grey or black instead. That single constraint is what stops the identity becoming a look.

What I would choose

Recommendation

Wordmark with the graft disclosed, as primary. Mark A (the paraph) as the symbol. Mark B held in reserve.

The wordmark does more conceptual work than any symbol can, and a project this literary can afford to lead with type. The paraph earns its place because it is the one glyph whose entire historical purpose was marking a second hand — and because it survives to 16 px, which brackets only just do. Mark B is the better choice if you ever need the identity to read as neutral scholarly apparatus rather than as an argument; keep it drawn.

Drop the manicule as a mark. Keep it as a glyph in the reader-side apparatus, where it is charming rather than load-bearing.

Open decisions

  1. Does the red hold up in monochrome? Papers get printed and photocopied. The seam becomes invisible and the paraph becomes ambiguous. Worth drawing a mono-safe variant before anything is locked — probably a weight difference rather than a tint.
  2. How literal do you want the manuscript reference? Right now it is a structural borrowing, not a costume: cool ground, contemporary faces, one historical convention. Pushing further towards vellum and Garamond would be warmer and much more generic.
  3. Should azurite exist yet? Reader-side is Phase 4. There is a case for shipping ink, quire and minium only, and introducing blue when the reader tooling actually exists — so the palette grows as the framework does.
  4. Where does the wordmark stop being a wordmark? If am. becomes the package name and the CLI, the graft never appears in daily use. Decide whether the short form keeps the red dot or drops the device entirely.

Next steps if you want them: mono-safe variants, a Mark B lockup family, or a live single-page site for amanuensimus.com built on this system.
