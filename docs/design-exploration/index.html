We did this.

The question was never whether a human typed it. It was whether anyone thought it through.

The objection

“If you don’t have time to write it, I don’t have time to read it.”

Time is the mask.

Labour used to be a proxy for thinking. Someone willing to spend a hundred hours on a piece of work had almost certainly spent time thinking about it. That inference was reliable for as long as it was expensive to be wrong.

AI decoupled labour from thinking, and the proxy broke. So the filter fell back to the last thing still measurable — does this look AI-generated? — which sorts on rendering rather than on thought. Wrong axis entirely.

What the objection means to say is: I don’t have time to read work whose author hasn’t thought about it. That is a fair thing to want. It is also much harder to measure, which is why almost nobody measures it.

The stance

Attribution follows responsibility, not the causal chain. Ideas were never wholly internal; the arranger who completes the pattern has always been the author. So ask the questions that actually discriminate.

  • Can you defend it under scrutiny you didn’t rehearse for?
  • Can you apply it to adjacent cases you never discussed with the model?
  • Do you understand its dependencies well enough to notice when they don’t hold?
  • Would you take responsibility for its consequences?

If yes, it is yours, and how it got made is philosophically uninteresting.

If no, you don’t have the idea. You have the appearance of one, and the gap will show under any competent questioning.

This is a stricter bar than I typed it myself, not a laxer one. The collaboration frame raises the accountability: the credit is yours, the labour was distributed, the consequences remain yours, and you had every opportunity to check. The model misled me is not a defence. It is evidence of failure to notice.

What is being built

Four pillars, in the order they became necessary.

  • Voice fidelity — generation that preserves an author’s voice, verified by measurement rather than asserted by prompt.
  • Disclosure integrity — every artefact carries a machine-readable provenance manifest that renders into human-readable disclosure.
  • Bias mitigation — evidence-first method and session hygiene, enforced by the tooling rather than left to discipline.
  • Reader-side integrity — the parallel practice for reading, because the writing norms are stranded without it.

The fourth is the one most likely to matter. New writing norms do not survive contact with old reading norms; everyone who accepts responsibility-based authorship stays marginal for as long as reading practice hasn’t caught up.

Where this is

Phase 0. Three documents and a domain. No code yet, deliberately — the position stabilises before anything is built on it.

This is a manifesto, not a product. It may grow into working technology, or into academic work, or into neither. That optionality is on purpose and is better stated than hidden.

It is meant to be argued with. If the position is wrong, or the practice insufficient, or the framing naïve, the response is revision rather than defence. Amendments are recorded on the other side of the rule.
