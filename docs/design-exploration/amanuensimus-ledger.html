amanuensimus Design ledger · pigment arc

The design is not versioned. It is amended — one token at a time, by the mechanism the argument already uses. The colour is not absent. It is withheld.

Why not a scale

A scale of design states forces coordinated jumps. Each rung has to land whole, every token moving at once, and judging it is the work of judging an entire redesign. That is expensive, brittle, and not how anything gets built.

Worse, it invites a schedule — which commits you to changing things before the reason has arrived. That is how a design acquires decisions nobody can account for.

Amendment, not version

An amendment moves one token. It carries a stated reason, a trigger, and its declared sympathies. Prior values are kept rather than overwritten.

Does the intermediate state hold up? then answers itself. Every state is a single delta from a state that already held, so there is nothing to synchronise: there is never more than one thing in motion.

What makes an amendment

  • One token. If it needs two unrelated tokens, it is two amendments.
  • A trigger, not a date. The condition under which the change becomes warranted.
  • A reason. Stated in terms of the work, not of taste.
  • Declared sympathies. The tokens needing a re-check once this one moves.

Sympathies, and deferring them

Some tokens cannot honestly move alone: change the face and the measure needs a look, because a different face sets a different number of characters to the em.

The answer is not to bundle them but to declare the coupling and apply the sympathy later, on its own evidence. Change the face; read it for a fortnight; shorten the measure because you have seen it fail, not because a rule predicted it would.

Why the colour was withheld

Two tones of one ink is rubrication with the pigment removed. Manuscripts marked the second hand in red; this marks it by weight.

But the history is more exact than the analogy. The rubricator worked last. The scribe wrote the body, left gaps, and a second person came afterwards with the red. A page in black alone is not a page that refused colour. It is a page the rubricator has not reached.

That is where this is. The pigment is declared and unspent, and it arrives when there is something to mark that weight alone cannot separate.

How the pigment arrives

  • By scope, one place at a time. Each expansion is an ordinary amendment with a trigger.
  • In marks, never in fields. No tinted panels, no coloured rules, no filled backgrounds. Rubric is small by construction, which is what keeps it quiet.
  • Semantically. Red is the second hand. Blue is the third. Ink is the first and never moves.
  • Not on the rule. The rule is not a hand. It is the claim that a division exists, and it stays in ink at every state — so the one element asserting a boundary is the only one that never acquires a voice.

Whether the record keeps its setting

Re-render everything at current tokens and the site stays coherent while the record of its own evolution is destroyed — which contradicts the position on abandoned versions directly.

Freeze every artefact at issue and the record survives, but the site becomes a patchwork of hands. That is either the point or a mess, and it is worth deciding which before there is an archive to regret.

The split that holds: the living statement renders at current tokens, because it is living. Dated artefacts freeze at issue. The top of the site is always now; the archive is stratified, and reading down it is watching the pigment arrive.

The cost is near zero. Tokens are custom properties, so freezing an artefact is one scoped declaration on its wrapper. Anti-erasure comes free, which is usually a sign the mechanism is the right shape.
